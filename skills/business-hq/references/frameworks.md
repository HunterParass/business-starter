# Frameworks cheat sheet

Short versions of the frameworks departments use. Use them as thinking tools, not as boxes to fill for the sake of it.

## Lean Canvas (Strategy)

Problem (top 3) / Customer segments (and early adopters) / Unique value proposition / Solution / Channels / Revenue streams / Cost structure / Key metrics / Unfair advantage. Mark each box proven, sourced or guessed.

## Jobs to be done (Customers)

"When **[situation]**, I want to **[motivation]**, so I can **[outcome]**."
Forces on a switch: **push** of the current situation, **pull** of the new solution, **anxiety** about the new thing, **habit** of the old way. A sale happens when push + pull beat anxiety + habit.

## The Mom Test (Customers)

1. Talk about their life, not your idea.
2. Ask about specific things in the past, not opinions about the future ("When did this last happen? What did you do? What did it cost?").
3. Talk less, listen more.
Good signals are commitments: time, money, reputation (an intro), or a next meeting. Compliments and "I'd totally use that" are noise.

## Positioning, April Dunford's sequence (Brand)

1. Competitive alternatives (what customers would really do instead)
2. Unique attributes you have that they lack
3. Value those attributes enable, with proof
4. Customers who care most about that value
5. Market category that makes the value obvious
6. Relevant trends (optional)
Onliness check: "We are the only ___ that ___ for ___."

## Bullseye and the 19 traction channels (Marketing)

From *Traction* (Weinberg and Mares): viral marketing, PR, unconventional PR, search engine marketing, social and display ads, offline ads, SEO, content marketing, email marketing, engineering as marketing (free tools), targeting blogs, business development (partnerships), sales, affiliate programs, existing platforms (app stores, marketplaces), trade shows, offline events, speaking engagements, community building.
Process: brainstorm all 19, rank, pick 3 to test cheaply in parallel, then focus on the 1 that works.

## AARRR funnel (Marketing, Finance)

Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue. Fix the leakiest stage first; usually retention.

## Pricing (Strategy)

- Three reference points: **cost-plus** is the floor, **competitor prices** are the anchor, **value to the customer** is the ceiling. Price toward the ceiling.
- Value metric: what the customer pays per (seat, order, project, month, unit) should grow with the value they get.
- Van Westendorp questions: at what price is it too cheap to trust, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive?
- Good / better / best tiers make the middle option the easy choice.
- Annual prepay (with a discount) brings cash in early.
- Raising prices later is harder than starting slightly high and discounting for early customers.

## MVP types (Product)

Concierge (do it by hand), wizard of oz (looks automated, is manual), landing page with a price and waitlist or pre-order, pre-sale, single-feature tool, no-code prototype, physical sample or small batch, a paid pilot.

## Experiment template (Product, Marketing)

Hypothesis: we believe **[segment]** will **[action]** because **[reason]**.
Test: **[method]** with **[sample size]** over **[time]**, costing **[money/hours]**.
Pass if: **[metric] >= [threshold]**, set before running.
If it fails: **[what we change]**.

## Risk matrix (Legal, Strategy, Red team)

Score = severity (1-5) x likelihood (1-5). 15+ act now, 8-14 mitigate before launch, under 8 monitor.

## Business model patterns (Strategy)

Subscription, freemium, one-time purchase, usage-based, marketplace take rate, transaction fee, productised service, agency or freelance, licensing, advertising or sponsorship, affiliate, in-app purchases or virtual goods, razor and blades, direct-to-consumer, wholesale, pre-order or crowdfunding, pay-what-you-want, franchise.

## Offer design: the value equation (Strategy, Sales)

Value feels higher when the **dream outcome** is bigger and the **perceived likelihood** of getting it is higher, and when the **time to result** and the **effort or sacrifice** are lower. Improve the offer by working on all four: proof and guarantees raise likelihood; done-for-you parts, templates and quick wins cut time and effort. Bonuses should remove specific objections. Urgency and scarcity only when real.

## Short-form video (Content)

- **Structure:** hook (first 1-2 seconds, spoken and on screen) -> payoff the hook promised -> one CTA. Cut every second that doesn't earn the next one.
- **Hook types:** curiosity gap ("I didn't expect this to work..."), result first (show the end, then how), problem call-out ("If your X keeps Y..."), contrarian ("Stop doing X"), story ("A customer messaged me at 2am..."), list ("3 things..."), POV, mistake, comparison, process ("Making 100 candles in one night").
- **Formats that work for small businesses:** making-of and process, packing orders, before and after, day in the life, tutorial, myth-busting, reply to a comment, customer reaction, founder story, behind the counter.
- **Loop:** post often, read the numbers weekly (hook hold rate, watch time, shares, saves, follows, clicks), re-cut winners with new hooks, test proven organic winners as paid ads.
- **Creators:** nano and micro creators with a real, matching audience usually beat big names for small budgets. Gifting, flat fee, commission or usage rights. Always disclosed.

## Inventory (Commerce, Finance)

- **Reorder point** = average daily sales x supplier lead time in days + safety stock.
- **Safety stock** (simple) = (highest daily sales - average daily sales) x lead time.
- Small batches and pre-orders beat big minimum orders until demand is proven. Dead stock is cash you can't spend.

## Referral loop (Retention)

Trigger moment (customer just got the result) -> easy share (link, code, card in the box) -> reward for both sides -> new customer gets a great first experience -> they hit their own trigger moment. Ask when people are happiest, not at checkout.

## Retail and wholesale pricing (Commerce)

Common starting rule: wholesale price about half of retail, and retail about double wholesale ("keystone"). Landed cost should be well under the wholesale price, or wholesale loses money once volume grows. Check what's normal in the specific category.

## Rules of thumb (verify for the specific industry)

These are widely quoted starting points, not laws. Departments should find a current, industry-specific source before relying on any of them.

- LTV:CAC of about 3:1 or better before scaling paid acquisition.
- CAC payback under about 12 months for subscription businesses.
- Software gross margins are typically high (often 70%+); physical products and services are much lower.
- A first-time founder with no audience rarely wins more than a few percent of a serviceable market in three years.
- Retention problems can't be fixed with more marketing.
